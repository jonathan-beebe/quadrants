# Skill × Will Matrix

The Skill/Will Matrix is a 2×2 coaching model that helps a manager choose an
interaction style for a specific person on a specific task. The horizontal axis
is **skill** (the person's capability at the task — experience, training,
demonstrated ability) and the vertical axis is **will** (their motivation and
desire to do it). The four quadrants each prescribe a management approach:
**Delegate** (high skill, high will), **Guide** (low skill, high will),
**Motivate** (high skill, low will — labelled _Excite_ in the original book),
and **Direct** (low skill, low will). The core idea is that there is no single
best management style; the right style is a function of where the person sits on
these two axes for the task at hand.

## When was it created?

The matrix was popularized in 1996, when Max Landsberg published it in
[_The Tao of Coaching_](https://archive.org/details/taoofcoachingboo0000land_i0m1)
(HarperCollins, 1996). The underlying two-factor idea is older: it descends from
Paul Hersey and Ken Blanchard's Situational Leadership model, first published in
1969 as the "Life Cycle Theory of Leadership" (see
[Wikipedia: Situational leadership theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situational_leadership_theory)).
So the honest dating is: axes from 1969, the "Skill/Will" packaging from 1996.

## Who created it?

[Max Landsberg](https://harpercollins.co.uk/blogs/authors/max-landsberg), a
former McKinsey & Company partner who built coaching programs for the firm and
its clients, formalized the Skill/Will Matrix as a named tool in _The Tao of
Coaching_. He did not invent the two-dimensional diagnosis from scratch — Hersey
and Blanchard's Situational Leadership model had already mapped follower
"readiness" (ability × willingness) to four leadership styles
([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situational_leadership_theory)).
Landsberg's contribution was to reframe it around the _employee_ rather than the
leader and compress it into a simple, memorable 2×2 with directly actionable
quadrant labels — Direct, Guide, Excite, Delegate
([Runn](https://www.runn.io/blog/skill-will-matrix),
[AIHR](https://www.aihr.com/blog/skill-will-matrix/)). Note that many modern
retellings of the matrix (including the "Motivate" quadrant label) are
paraphrases; the book's own labels use "Excite"
([book excerpt, Waterstones PDF](https://cdn.waterstones.com/special/pdf/9781781253328.pdf)).

## What problem were they trying to solve?

Managers tend to have one default style — some micromanage everyone, some
delegate to everyone — and applying that default uniformly fails predictably:
micromanaging a skilled, motivated person demoralizes them, while delegating to
an unskilled or unmotivated person sets them up to fail. Landsberg, writing from
his experience establishing coaching at McKinsey, wanted a tool a busy manager
could apply in day-to-day interactions to _diagnose before prescribing_: first
assess this person's skill and will for this task, then match the coaching style
to the diagnosis ([Runn](https://www.runn.io/blog/skill-will-matrix),
[The Tao of Coaching](https://archive.org/details/taoofcoachingboo0000land_i0m1)).

## Why should someone use it?

- It replaces a one-size-fits-all management style with a cheap, fast diagnostic
  that fits in your head during a 1:1.
- It separates two failure modes that look similar from the outside — "can't do
  it" (skill problem, needs training/guidance) versus "won't do it" (will
  problem, needs motivation, purpose, or removed blockers) — which call for
  opposite responses.
- It is task-specific, not a personality label: the same person can be
  "Delegate" on one task and "Guide" on another, which keeps the tool humane and
  avoids pigeonholing ([AIHR](https://www.aihr.com/blog/skill-will-matrix/)).
- For self-management, it works in reverse: knowing your own quadrant for a task
  tells you what kind of support to ask for.

## What is the expected outcome?

Used as intended, the manager's style matches each person's actual needs, so
skilled/motivated people get autonomy (and stay engaged), motivated novices get
structured guidance (and grow skill), skilled-but-disengaged people get their
motivation problem addressed rather than more training, and low-skill/low-will
situations get close direction with clear expectations. The aggregate outcome
Landsberg promises is a team that develops over time toward the Delegate
quadrant — higher capability, higher motivation, and a manager who spends less
time firefighting
([The Tao of Coaching](https://archive.org/details/taoofcoachingboo0000land_i0m1),
[Runn](https://www.runn.io/blog/skill-will-matrix)).

## What is the hard evidence it works?

Direct evidence is thin: no dedicated peer-reviewed studies validate the
Skill/Will Matrix itself, despite its wide adoption in management practice. The
evidence that exists attaches to its parent model and to coaching in general:

- The parent Situational Leadership model has been researched for decades with
  mixed results. A critical review in _The Leadership Quarterly_ concluded that
  the theory remains among the less well-substantiated leadership models, with
  inconsistent empirical support and conceptual ambiguity in how "readiness" is
  defined
  ([Graeff, 1997, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S104898439790014X)).
  Studies in military settings were inconclusive about the predicted interaction
  between leader style and follower readiness
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situational_leadership_theory)).
- Workplace coaching in general has stronger support: the 2016 Jones, Woods &
  Guillaume meta-analysis found structured coaching interventions produced
  significant positive effects on workplace performance and learning outcomes
  ([WorkHuman summary](https://www.workhuman.com/blog/coaching-models/)).
- Adjacent skills-focused practice has favorable survey data: Deloitte's 2022
  research found skills-based organizations 63% more likely to achieve results,
  107% more likely to place talent effectively, and 98% more likely to retain
  high performers
  ([Salary.com summary](https://www.salary.com/resources/hr-glossary/what-is-skill-will-matrix-example-and-free-template)).

In short: the diagnostic habit the matrix encodes (adapt style to the person) is
plausible and coaching works, but the 2×2 itself is a practitioner heuristic,
not a validated instrument
([PerformYard](https://www.performyard.com/articles/skill-will-matrix)).

## How does one use it properly?

1. **Pick one person and one task.** Assess skill (capability, experience,
   demonstrated ability) and will (motivation, enthusiasm, commitment to the
   goal) for that specific task, not the person in general
   ([Runn](https://www.runn.io/blog/skill-will-matrix),
   [AIHR](https://www.aihr.com/blog/skill-will-matrix/)).
2. **Anchor the assessment in observables.** Rate against KPIs, work samples,
   and performance data rather than intuition, and talk with the person to
   validate the read; account for external factors affecting performance
   ([Runn](https://www.runn.io/blog/skill-will-matrix)).
3. **Plot the quadrant and match the style.** **Delegate**: grant autonomy,
   stretch goals, visibility. **Guide**: structured learning, close early
   feedback, gradually widening scope. **Excite/Motivate**: uncover what is
   draining motivation, reconnect the work to purpose, remove blockers.
   **Direct**: clear instructions, short feedback loops, extensive support
   ([Runn](https://www.runn.io/blog/skill-will-matrix)).
4. **Run the coaching conversation with a structured model** such as GROW (Goal,
   Reality, Options, Wrap-Up) so the diagnosis turns into an agreed development
   plan ([Runn](https://www.runn.io/blog/skill-will-matrix)).
5. **Reassess on a regular cadence.** People move quadrants as skill and
   motivation change; refresh the read at 1:1s and fold it into performance
   reviews so there is a visible growth path, alongside goals and feedback
   systems ([Runn](https://www.runn.io/blog/skill-will-matrix),
   [PerformYard](https://www.performyard.com/articles/skill-will-matrix)).

## How does it relate to other frameworks?

- **Situational Leadership** (Hersey & Blanchard, 1969) is the parent: it maps
  the same ability × willingness diagnosis to four leader behaviors (telling,
  selling, participating, delegating)
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situational_leadership_theory)).
- **GROW**, **CLEAR**, **FUEL**, and **OSKAR** are structured coaching-
  conversation models; Skill/Will diagnoses the person's state, and these
  frameworks then structure the conversation that follows — GROW is the most
  common pairing and has the most published research support
  ([WorkHuman](https://www.workhuman.com/blog/coaching-models/),
  [Tandem Coach](https://www.tandemcoach.co/coaching-frameworks/)).
- [Passion × Proficiency](./passion-proficiency.md) is the self-directed mirror
  image: the same two axes applied to your own activities instead of a report's
  tasks.
- [Competence Ladder](./competence-ladder.md) describes the stages a person
  moves through as skill grows — useful for seeing motion along the skill axis
  rather than a snapshot.
- [Love / Loathe / Learn / Leave](./love-loathe-learn-leave.md) crosses
  competence with energy for job crafting; it reinforces the will axis by
  showing which skilled work is actually draining.
- [Power × Interest](./power-interest.md) is the analogous person-by-person 2×2
  for stakeholders: plot each individual on two dimensions, then vary your
  engagement style by quadrant.

## What is the core insight?

"Can't do it" and "won't do it" look identical from the outside but demand
opposite responses, so diagnose a person's skill and will for the task before
choosing how to manage them.

## What is its intellectual-property status?

The Skill/Will Matrix is free to use: Landsberg never trademarked or patented
it, and training firms publish templates and guides without licensing
restrictions ([AIHR](https://www.aihr.com/blog/skill-will-matrix/)). Two nearby
marks exist but do not constrain the framework. The parent model's name is
protected — the Center for Leadership Studies owns Situational Leadership® and
related marks, and Ken Blanchard Companies retained SLII® after their 2018 IP
settlement
([CLS press release](https://situational.com/press-releases/the-center-for-leadership-studies-and-the-ken-blanchard-companies-resolve-intellectual-property-litigation/))
— so avoid branding derivative material "Situational Leadership." A separate
"SKILL AND WILL" trademark (Reg. #5478961, Sales Pro Insider, Inc., 2018) covers
an unrelated sales-training product
([Trademarkia](https://www.trademarkia.com/skill-and-will-87454956)). The book
_The Tao of Coaching_ remains under copyright; the matrix concept and quadrant
labels are freely reproducible.
