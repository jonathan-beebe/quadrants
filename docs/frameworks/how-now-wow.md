# How–Now–Wow Matrix

The How–Now–Wow matrix is a 2×2 idea-selection tool used at the convergent stage
of brainstorming. Ideas are plotted by _originality_ on one axis and _ease of
implementation_ on the other, producing three named categories plus a discard
quadrant: **Now** (ordinary ideas that are easy to implement — incremental
improvements), **Wow** (original ideas that are also easy to implement — the
breakthrough sweet spot), **How** (original ideas that are not yet feasible —
future potential worth revisiting), and the unoriginal, infeasible leftovers
(labeled **Ciao** in some modern variants, including this app's template). It is
the popular name for the COCD Box, a method from the Belgian creative-thinking
tradition, and its purpose is to break the "creadox" (creative paradox): groups
brainstorm wild ideas and then reflexively pick the safest, most familiar ones
([Gamestorming](https://gamestorming.com/how-now-wow-matrix/)).

## When was it created?

The exact creation date of the COCD Box is not well documented in public
sources. Its parent institution, COCD (Centrum voor de Ontwikkeling van het
Creatief Denken — Center for the Development of Creative Thinking), was founded
in 1977 as a spin-off of the University of Antwerp in Belgium
([School of Creative Thinking](https://schoolofcreativethinking.nl/en/school/));
some secondary sources conflate that founding date with the creation of the box
itself, which is almost certainly wrong.
[Toolshero](https://www.toolshero.com/creativity/cocd-box-method/) cites the
method as "(Raison, 1997)", suggesting a 1990s origin. The method reached a wide
English-speaking audience via the book _Creativity Today_ by Igor Byttebier and
Ramon Vullings (BIS Publishers, 2007) and via the "How-Now-Wow Matrix" write-up
in [Gamestorming](https://gamestorming.com/how-now-wow-matrix/) (based on the
2010 book by Gray, Brown, and Macanufo), which credits _Creativity Today_ as its
source. A fair summary: developed within COCD sometime between the 1980s and
1990s, popularized internationally from 2007 onward.

## Who created it?

The COCD Box is most commonly attributed to
[Mark Raison](https://www.toolshero.com/creativity/cocd-box-method/), a
creativity facilitator who developed it for COCD; Toolshero states plainly that
"the method was developed by Mark Raison, an expert in creative processes and
guiding teams in generating and selecting ideas."
[Gamestorming](https://gamestorming.com/how-now-wow-matrix/) attributes it
institutionally rather than personally, saying the matrix "is adapted from work
done by The Center for Development of Creative Thinking (COCD)" and was
published in _Creativity Today_ by Ramon Vullings, Igor Byttebier, and Godelieve
Spaas. COCD itself was founded by University of Antwerp professors Pros
Vanosmael and Roger De Bruyn
([School of Creative Thinking](https://schoolofcreativethinking.nl/en/school/)),
but they are credited with the institution, not this specific tool. Honest
attribution: Mark Raison / COCD created it; Byttebier, Vullings, and the
Gamestorming authors popularized it and gave it the How–Now–Wow name recognition
it has today.

## What problem were they trying to solve?

Two linked problems in group creativity. First, organizations were often good at
_generating_ ideas but had no structured way to _evaluate and select_ them, so
promising ideas died in unstructured discussion
([Toolshero](https://www.toolshero.com/creativity/cocd-box-method/)). Second,
the "creadox": when groups converge on ideas, "people often end up picking ideas
that are most familiar to them" — the safest, least original options — so the
originality generated during brainstorming evaporates at the selection step
([Gamestorming](https://gamestorming.com/how-now-wow-matrix/)). By forcing every
idea to be judged on originality _as well as_ feasibility, the matrix protects
innovative ideas from being rejected merely because they aren't immediately
implementable.

## Why should someone use it?

Because unstructured idea selection systematically kills the best ideas. The
matrix makes the selection criteria explicit and two-dimensional, so a group can
honor incremental wins (Now), commit to genuine breakthroughs (Wow), and —
crucially — _keep_ bold-but-infeasible ideas visible as future work (How)
instead of discarding them. It is fast, visual, needs no training, works with
sticky notes or dot-voting in any workshop, and gives quieter, less politically
safe ideas an equal footing with familiar ones. In the original COCD color
coding, these are the blue (feasible, ordinary), green/red (feasible and
original), and yellow (original, not yet feasible) idea categories
([Toolshero](https://www.toolshero.com/creativity/cocd-box-method/)).

## What is the expected outcome?

A brainstorm's raw output sorted into an actionable portfolio: a set of **Now**
ideas to implement immediately for incremental benefit, one or a few **Wow**
ideas selected as the innovation bets that combine originality with
achievability, and a preserved backlog of **How** ideas to revisit as
technology, budget, or constraints change — with clearly weak ideas consciously
dropped. Used as intended, the group leaves the session with more original ideas
surviving selection than an unstructured vote would produce, and with shared,
explicit reasoning about why each idea was chosen or parked
([Gamestorming](https://gamestorming.com/how-now-wow-matrix/),
[SessionLab](https://www.sessionlab.com/methods/how-now-wow-matrix)).
